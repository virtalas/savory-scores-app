How to Tell if a Japanese Restaurant Is Authentic: 8 Signs

Savory Scores guide · Helsinki · Updated October 2026

"Authentic" gets thrown around a lot. In practice it means food that a person from Japan would recognise and enjoy as the real thing. You can usually tell before the food even arrives.

8 signs you're in an authentic Japanese restaurant

  1. The menu is short and focused. A ramen shop that also serves pizza and pad thai isn't a ramen shop.
  2. Dishes use their Japanese names: oyakodon, karaage, tonkatsu. The menu doesn't just say "chicken rice bowl".
  3. There are set meals (teishoku) with rice, miso soup and small sides. That's how people eat everyday Japanese food.
  4. Rice is short-grain, glossy and plain. It doesn't come drenched in sauce, and isn't fried unless the dish is literally fried rice.
  5. Sushi isn't mostly rolls covered in mayonnaise and sweet sauce. Nigiri and sashimi lead the menu.
  6. Cooked food goes beyond sushi. Curry, donburi, udon, tempura and grilled fish show the kitchen knows home-style cooking.
  7. You'll find Japanese drinks: green tea, sake, shochu, Japanese beer.
  8. Japanese customers eat there. It's the oldest trick in the book, and it still works.

Authentic doesn't automatically mean better

Plenty of excellent dishes are fusion, and some very traditional plates are just okay. That's why Savory Scores keeps taste and authenticity separate. You can look for a gyudon that tastes like Yoshinoya at home, or simply the most delicious one in town, and get two different answers.

How to rank Japanese food by authenticity with Savory Scores

  1. Search a dish category, for example Gyudon.
  2. Change the sort from Score to Authenticity. The list reorders so the most traditional versions come first, each with its own authenticity dial.
  3. Open "Read about Gyudon" to learn what the traditional version looks like, so you know what to expect.
  4. Add your own review. If you know the dish from Japan, your authenticity rating is exactly what other diners need.
