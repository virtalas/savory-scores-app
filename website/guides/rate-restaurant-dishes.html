How to Rate Restaurant Dishes (and Always Remember What to Order)

Savory Scores guide · Helsinki · Updated October 2026

You loved the tempura there. Or was it the place across the street? Most of us forget which dishes were great within a few weeks. A food rating habit fixes that, as long as it takes seconds, not minutes.

Rate the dish, not the restaurant

Restaurant star ratings mix everything together: service, decor, price, a long wait. They can't tell you what to order. Dish ratings can. The same restaurant can have a 9/10 ramen and a 4/10 katsudon, and that's exactly the information you need next time.

Why most food diaries fail

  • They take too long. Writing notes at the table is awkward, so you stop doing it.
  • Five-star scales drift. Is this a 4 or a 5? Without context your scores mean different things on different days.
  • You can't compare. A list of notes doesn't tell you which of six tempuras was the best.

The one-question system

Savory Scores asks a single question after a meal: Would you have it again?

  • Nope…: once was enough.
  • Maybe?: fine, but not a reason to come back.
  • Yeah!: I'd order this again.

It's fast enough to answer while the plates are still on the table, and honest in a way a star rating isn't.

How to rate dishes with Savory Scores

  1. Tap + and pick the restaurant. It links to the website and Maps automatically.
  2. Choose the dish, such as Tempura no moriawase. It's filed under its food category (Tempura).
  3. Answer: Nope, Maybe or Yeah. Done. Or skip if you just want to log it.
  4. Fine-tune if you like. A slider turns your answer into a score out of 10, with your previous tempura scores shown right below, so an 8 always means the same thing.
  5. Add the portion size (small, medium or big) so you know what to expect next time.

After a few meals you get a personal top list for every category, and your ratings show up the next time you search, so you'll never order the Nope twice.
